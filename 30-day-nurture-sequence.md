# The Speed to Lead Audit: 30-day nurture sequence

For everyone who completes the audit and does not book straight away. Thirty emails, sequenced across roughly thirty days. Cadence is front-loaded (daily in week one, when the audit is freshest in their mind) and spaces out from there. Suggested send days are a starting point, adjust to what your list tolerates.

Every email uses `{{contact.first_name}}`. Emails 1 and 2 also use `{{contact.speed_to_lead_score}}`, `{{contact.speed_to_lead_max}}` and `{{contact.speed_to_lead_band}}`, and should be split into three branch variants (high, mid, low) inside the GHL workflow, as described in the setup guide. From Email 3 onward the copy is written to work for any band, since most contacts will already know their score from Emails 1 and 2.

Add the exit condition described in the setup guide: stop the sequence the moment someone books a Growth Review.

All copy follows the Amigo voice rules: Australian English, no em dashes, no hype, no jargon.

### Email 3, Day 3: The five-minute rule

**Subject:** Why five minutes is the number that matters

**Body:**
Hi {{contact.first_name}},

Speed to lead research keeps landing on the same number: five minutes. Respond within five minutes and your odds of actually connecting with that lead jump dramatically. Wait thirty minutes and those odds fall off a cliff.

Most businesses are not losing leads because their offer is weak. They are losing them because someone else replied first. The business that answers first usually wins the conversation, not the business with the better price.

If your audit flagged gaps in section one, speed to lead, that is the first thing worth fixing. It is also usually the fastest to fix, because it does not require a bigger team. It requires a better first response, automated so it never depends on someone being at their desk.

## Week two: inside each gap

### Email 8, Day 9: After hours is not a nice-to-have

**Subject:** What happens to leads after 5pm

**Body:**
Hi {{contact.first_name}},

A large share of enquiries for service businesses come in outside standard hours: evenings, weekends, whenever someone finally has a free moment to think about hiring you.

If those leads sit untouched until 9am Monday, you are not just slow. You are invisible during the exact window a lot of people are actually deciding. By the time you reply, they may have already booked with whoever answered that night.

You do not need a night shift to fix this. You need an automated first response that keeps the conversation warm until a real person can pick it up in the morning. That is a system, not a staffing problem.

Mazzy

### Email 9, Day 11: The robot problem

**Subject:** Automated does not have to mean robotic

**Body:**
Hi {{contact.first_name}},

A common worry I hear about automating follow-up: "I don't want my leads to feel like they're talking to a robot."

Fair concern. Badly built automation does feel robotic, and that costs you trust exactly when you are trying to build it. Well built automation reads like a person who happens to always be available: it asks a real question, uses the lead's own words back to them, and hands off to your team the moment a human touch actually matters.

The goal was never to replace your team on the conversation. It is to make sure nobody waits for it.

Mazzy

### Email 10, Day 13: Qualifying before the call

**Subject:** Stop finding out on the call that it's not a fit

**Body:**
Hi {{contact.first_name}},

How many discovery calls have you or your team taken that turned out to be a poor fit within the first two minutes? That is time your team will never get back, and it happens because nobody asked the right questions before the call was booked.

A good automated conversation asks two or three qualifying questions upfront: what they need, their timeline, sometimes budget. By the time a real person joins the conversation, they already know whether this is worth their full attention.

It is a small change with a large effect on how much of your team's time actually goes toward leads that convert.

Mazzy

### Email 11, Day 15: The CRM gap

**Subject:** If it's not in your CRM, it doesn't exist

**Body:**
Hi {{contact.first_name}},

A conversation that never makes it into your CRM is a conversation your business cannot learn from, follow up on later, or hand to someone else if the original person is away. It just disappears the moment the chat window closes.

If section five of your audit, conversion readiness, flagged this, it is worth fixing early. Connecting conversations to your CRM and pipeline means every enquiry becomes a trackable opportunity, not a text thread sitting on someone's personal phone.

This is also where automatic follow-up becomes possible: if a lead goes quiet, the system can nudge them again without anyone having to remember to.

Mazzy

### Email 12, Day 17: Booking without the back and forth

**Subject:** Every "what times work for you" costs you a lead

**Body:**
Hi {{contact.first_name}},

Every extra step between "I'm interested" and "I've got a time booked in" is a chance for someone to lose momentum and drift off. The classic offender: three or four messages just to agree on a time.

If a lead can book directly from the conversation, that friction disappears. They pick a time, it lands in your calendar, done. No back and forth, no waiting on a reply that might come tomorrow instead of today.

Small change. Consistently one of the highest-leverage fixes in section five of the audit.

Mazzy

### Email 13, Day 19: When your team should step in

**Subject:** Automation gets them to the door. Your team closes it.

**Body:**
Hi {{contact.first_name}},

Automation is not meant to replace a real conversation with your team. It is meant to make sure a real conversation actually happens, instead of the lead going cold while everyone is busy.

The best setups I build hand off cleanly: automation handles the instant reply, the qualifying questions, and getting a time on the calendar. Your team steps in for the part that actually needs a human, the sales conversation itself, with full context already gathered.

That handoff is where most DIY automation setups fall down. It is also the part worth getting right, since it is the difference between a system that helps your team and one that just adds noise.
